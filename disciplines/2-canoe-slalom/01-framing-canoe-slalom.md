# Framing Sheet — Canoe Slalom
**Week 37 · Aug 31-Sep 6, 2026 · LA2028**

## 1. What "performance" means in this discipline
Canoe Slalom is a timed race down an artificial rapids course (roughly 200-400m) marked with **18 to 25 gates**, at least 6 of which must be negotiated against the current. Gates are numbered and must be passed in strict order: gates negotiated in the normal downstream direction are typically marked **green and white**, while gates requiring an upstream, reverse-direction approach are typically marked **red and white**. Final score = **race time + penalty seconds**:
- **+2 seconds** for touching a gate pole while still negotiating it correctly.
- **+50 seconds** for missing a gate entirely, negotiating it in the wrong direction, or displacing it by 45 degrees.
- The lowest total score wins.

Format: heats (best of two runs) → semifinal (one run) → final (one run). New at Paris 2024 and continuing at LA28: **Kayak Cross**, a head-to-head knockout format (replacing the old "Extreme K1") where 4 athletes race simultaneously on a shortened course.

There are **6 Olympic events**: Men's and Women's Canoe Single (C1), Kayak Single (K1), and Kayak Cross.

## 2. LA2028 qualification calendar
- **Total quota**: 82 athlete places for Canoe Slalom (out of 318 total for Canoeing, alongside Canoe Sprint).
- **Official qualification window**: opens July 20, 2026, closes October 4, 2027 (Olympic Ranking phase).
- **Qualification pathways** (three-phase system):
  - **Olympic Ranking**: 38 places (19 men / 19 women) allocated via ICF-sanctioned competitions over the 2026-2027 window (best 5 results out of 9 counted).
  - **Continental qualifiers**: 30 places, split evenly men/women.
  - **Global qualifier**: 12 places — the top 2 highest-ranked, not-yet-qualified athletes per relevant competition earn a quota for their NOC.
  - **Host nation (USA)**: 2 automatic slalom places.
- **Olympic competition**: Games run July 14-30, 2028, but **canoe slalom is hosted in Oklahoma City** (Riversport OKC), not in Los Angeles itself — an important detail for venue-specific data (course conditions, altitude, water flow rate).

## 4. Performance indicators to track (leads for the analysis)
- Raw run time vs. penalty-adjusted time (technical risk-taking vs. clean execution trade-off)
- Penalty rate per run (average seconds of penalty incurred per competition)
- Consistency across heats/semifinal/final (variance in an athlete's own times)
- Progression of best time on recurring venues (e.g., repeated World Cup courses)
- Performance gap between Olympic Ranking results and Continental Qualifier results (context/pressure effect)

## 6. Notes / research hypotheses
- Canoe slalom has been an Olympic event since 1992 (with a gap after a 1972 debut), giving many seasons of comparable results to build trend analysis on.
- Course-specific effects (water flow, gate setup difficulty) are a major confound — always compare times within the same competition/course setup rather than across different venues.
- Kayak Cross is new enough (since Paris 2024) that historical data for it is very limited — worth noting as its own sub-case with a smaller sample than the traditional time-trial events.
